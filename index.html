01 · Why agents alone fail

Flexibility without rails turns into lottery.

Coding agents (Claude Code, Codex and the like) build a first version fast — and then solve the same task differently every day. For a business process that runs hundreds of times, that is unacceptable. The math is brutal:

20 steps × 95% reliability per step = 0.95²⁰ ≈ 36% successful runs end-to-end. Even at 99% per step you only get ≈ 82%. Serious automation therefore puts only narrow tasks inside the neural network — and keeps everything else on fixed rails.

What we never automate

  • Auto-sending and deleting emails
  • Booking travel and charging cards unsupervised
  • Purchases made by an agent's own hands
  • Mass auto-replies to candidates or clients

What AI does brilliantly

  • Morning briefings and “read it, suggest a draft” mail
  • Monitoring — “write me when X changes”
  • Files, receipts and price lists → tidy tables
  • Call transcripts → notes → straight into the CRM

02 · The method — engineer & rails

Two parts. Each does what it is best at.

This is the architecture the whole market converged on in 2026 — OpenAI, Zapier and n8n all landed on the same formula: an agent on top, a predictable process below.

  1. The engineer (non-deterministic agent)

    Builds your automation from a plain-language brief in days, not months. Then re-reads the run journal every week and proposes small fixes — which you approve.

  2. The rails (n8n workflows + code + database)

    A fixed sequence: accept the request, route it, save it, remind on schedule. Runs exactly as specified — a hundred or a thousand times in a row. This is why n8n, not a chat window, sits at the center.

  3. The conductor (an LLM step inside the car)

    One narrow job per call — classify the request, extract dates and amounts, draft a reply. Rigid input format, rigid output format. The train never turns off the rails.

  4. The gateway (LiteLLM) and the journal (Postgres)

    Every model call goes through your own gateway: routing between cheap and smart models, cost control, dashboards. Every run logs input, output, errors, cost and time.

  5. Durable execution

    If step 7 of 10 fails at 3 a.m., the run resumes from step 7 — not from the start. No double charges, no duplicate letters to clients, and an approval can wait a week without breaking anything.

03 · Three industries, three working rails

Not theory — the three most-requested setups.

Drawn from industry data (Anthropic, Zapier, McKinsey, Menlo Ventures) and hundreds of real deployments: these are the processes companies automate first, because the payoff is fastest and the risk is lowest.

INDUSTRY 1 · HOME SERVICES, CLINICS, EDUCATION

Lead intake & follow-up that never sleeps

A request arrives at 11 p.m. from a form or Telegram. By morning the client is cold — or already booked with a competitor. The rails accept the request instantly, the LLM fills in a tidy client card from free text, and your manager gets a ready-to-send reply with Send / Edit buttons. Every day at 10:00 the system drafts follow-ups for everyone who went quiet 2+ days ago — again, only with approval. As practitioners put it: “the biggest change wasn't an AI tool — it was boring automated follow-ups.”

form / Telegram → n8n rails → LLM fills card → Postgres leads + runs → manager: Send / Edit → client, only after “yes”

Result: first reply in under a minute, zero lost evening requests, +20–40% of quiet leads reactivated by follow-ups.

INDUSTRY 2 · ONLINE RETAIL & E-COMMERCE

First-line support: instant, but never unsupervised

“Where is my order?”, “does this fit?”, “how do I return it?” — 70%+ of tickets are lookups, not decisions. The rails pull the order from your store, the LLM drafts the answer from your knowledge base, and anything uncertain — refunds, complaints, anger — lands in a human queue as a prepared ticket. The Klarna lesson of 2024–2025 (full autopilot, quality collapse, staff hired back) is designed out from day one.

chat / email → n8n rails → order + FAQ lookup → LLM drafts answer → confident? send · unsure? human queue

Result: instant answers 24/7 for routine questions, agents handle only the cases that need people — support cost per ticket drops several-fold.

INDUSTRY 3 · ACCOUNTING & LEGAL FIRMS

Invoices and documents with an exception queue

Extracting amounts, dates and counterparties from scans is exactly the narrow task LLMs do well — and exactly where blind trust is dangerous. The rails parse every document, and anything below ~97% confidence goes to your specialist in an exception queue instead of into the books. Humans stop typing and start verifying.

scan / PDF → n8n rails → LLM extracts fields → confidence ≥ 97%? → into ledger · else → exception queue

Result: routine documents processed in seconds, a clean audit trail for every figure, specialists focused on judgment — not data entry.

05 · FAQ

Honest answers.

Is n8n dead? Everyone talks about agents now.

No — its role changed. In 2026 n8n raised at a $5.2B valuation and shipped Agents, where a workflow becomes the agent's tool: it “executes exactly as specified.” ChatGPT's agent builder closed; Zapier plugged the agent back into the ordinary step-by-step flow. The market verdict: an agent on top, a predictable process below.

Will the AI send something stupid to a client?

Not through our rails. Anything customer-facing waits for a human tap — Send or Edit in Telegram. The LLM reads and drafts; the rails and your manager decide. Fully autonomous sending is on our never-automate list.

Where do my data and models live?

On your server: n8n, the database and the LiteLLM gateway run in Docker on your VPS. Model calls can be routed to any provider — or a European / local one if compliance requires it. No data passes through our infrastructure after handover.

What does it cost to run?

A base setup fits a small VPS (2 CPU / 4 GB RAM, a few euros a month). Model costs are capped per workflow in the gateway — typically cents per lead or ticket — and every cent is visible in the journal.

We already tried a chatbot and it flopped. How is this different?

A chatbot improvises the whole conversation. Our LLM step does one narrow job with a strict format inside a fixed pipeline — and a human approves the outcome. That is the difference between a demo and a process that survives contact with real clients.
